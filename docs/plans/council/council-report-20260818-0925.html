Council Verdict: Agent v2 Architecture Plan

5 advisors, 5 anonymous peer reviews, chairman synthesis · 2026-08-18
Question: Is the agent v2 architecture plan (design laws L1-L12, migration map M0-M7, deep-dive roadmap R0-R6) the right plan, correctly sequenced, for making EcomGear's agent flagship-grade? What should change before executing the remaining stages?

Where the Council Agrees

  • The evidence base is inverted. All six confirmed failures of the past week were platform/harness failures; zero were architecture. The plan prescribes an agent redesign for what the record says is an instrumentation deficit. (4 of 5 advisors independently; all 5 reviewers endorsed.)
  • Two roadmaps must become one. M0-M7 and R0-R6 conflict about what ships next; "R6: M2-M7 as previously mapped" is a merge conflict, not a sequence. Publish one ordered list.
  • Customer projects living in /tmp is a live data-loss bug, not an "ops decision blocking M1b." Unanimous, and it is the plan's worst mislabel.
  • Cut PID-controlled provider balancing. It solves >1B tokens/min; you run ~20 tenants. Twenty lines of static per-project cache affinity gets 90%.
  • M0/instrumentation is the real next stage and is under-specified relative to its actual importance.

Where the Council Clashes

  • Vent tool: week one or never? Four advisors rank it first (cheapest compounding instrument); the Contrarian says a solo founder cannot consume the queue. Peer review broke the tie with data: only 1 of the 6 real incidents was visible from inside a run, so the vent tool would have caught ~1/6. It stays, one day of work, but demoted from savior to supplement.
  • Is the architecture work valuable at all? Contrarian/Outsider/First-Principles: not now. Expansionist: it is revenue (git store = agency export; route map = four features). Executor: fine, just resequence. Chairman's resolution: deferred, not dead, and gated on instruments existing.
  • R2 SEO bundle: pricing-tier opportunity (Expansionist) vs Lovable-copying (Outsider). Executor settles it: hours of Caddy/publish work, detached from agent risk, so it runs as a parallel track regardless of the framing.

Blind Spots the Council Caught (peer review only)

  • The synthetic canary beats the vent tool and nobody proposed it as the centerpiece: an end-to-end scripted run acting as a real customer against production (create → edit → preview → reload → publish, asserting on served bytes) would have caught 5-6 of the 6 incidents. The vent tool catches ~1.
  • No migration-safety story for live tenants. M1b moves customer files, M4 replaces the edit engine, M5 rewrites finalize, all while paying customers are mid-session. No rollback path, no shadow period, no per-stage abort criterion anywhere in either roadmap.
  • The /tmp fix is itself the risky part: a live migration with open file handles. Step one is snapshot + integrity-check, then move, with rollback.
  • Nothing is falsifiable: no size estimates, no time budgets, no kill criteria, and the acceptance test is n=1 (the logo saga). The plan cannot currently be shown wrong.
  • Zero customer evidence: every justification traces to Lovable, none to CardPro or 38 Digital.

The Recommendation

Keep the design laws, freeze the architecture stages, and rebuild the roadmap around observability. One ordered list:

  • Day 0: snapshot + integrity-check every project under /tmp, migrate appPath to persistent disk with a rollback path. Merge M-map and R-map into one document; delete the other.
  • Weeks 1-2, the Nervous System (new M0): the synthetic customer canary (interval + post-deploy, asserts on served bytes), guard/validator unit tests, the trace scoreboard, the vent tool as a one-day supplement, and a kill-criterion + rollback template every later stage must fill in. Parallel track: R2 SEO bundle (hours, detached, revenue-bearing).
  • Then M2-M7 one stage at a time, each gated on: canary green, a replay corpus grown from real traces (not n=1), a size estimate, and a revert criterion. Order M2 before R2-dependent work (the route map feeds four features). Cut R5's PID balancer; keep 20-line static affinity.
  • Before choosing M2 vs M4 order: ask CardPro and 38 Digital for their top three complaints. It is the only churn-predicting evidence available and it is currently absent from the plan.
The one thing to do first: today, snapshot and integrity-check every customer project under /tmp on VPS3, then move appPath to persistent disk with a tested rollback. Every other line of both roadmaps assumes customer data survives a reboot, and right now it does not.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — halt the architecture work
Two roadmaps that disagree about what month it is; pick one and delete the other today. The evidence does not support an architecture rewrite: every confirmed failure was harness or infrastructure, not context-engine or edit-format. R1 is the only item the incidents actually generated. appPath under /tmp holds live customer projects right now: an active data-loss bug ranked nowhere. Lovable is being copied at a scale you do not have (PID balancing solves >1B tokens/min; the vent queue needs a triage org). Copy the SSR/prerender finding, drop R5. The acceptance test is n=1; ask CardPro what actually churns them before spending a month guessing.
The First Principles Thinker — build the nervous system
The last month of pain was "we shipped something broken and did not find out for weeks", six times. One root cause: no signal distinguishing "the model did badly" from "our platform lied to the model." Until that exists, every stage ships into fog and each new mechanism (M3/M4/M5) is another guard that can silently punish the model: six new failure surfaces on a system whose existing surfaces failed undetected. "Lovable-competitive" is the wrong target; the real asset is you can talk to CardPro today. M0 is the entire product for the next two weeks and should absorb R1 plus the vent tool. appPath in /tmp is a live data-loss bug: fix it today.
The Expansionist — the hidden revenue surfaces
The vent tool compounds: every paying customer's run becomes unstaffed QA; move it to R1. The route map is four features (context, prerender target list, smoke router, sitemap) so build it once at M2 and R2 gets cheaper. R2 is a pricing tier, not parity: "sites AI search engines can actually read" is a line item nobody at this tier ships, and Lovable measured +98.5% AI-crawler lift from prerendering alone. The git store is really the agency export path (GitHub sync, PR handoff) and 38 Digital is an agency. Publish "cost per verified change" as the marketing story.
The Outsider — the fresh-eyes read
The plan is written in a private language: two documents, three numbering systems, one superseding another mid-flight; a stranger cannot say what ships next week. Nothing comes from a customer; every justification is "Lovable does X." The plan answers last week's platform disasters with an agent redesign none of them needed. PID balancing at ~20 tenants is a capacity strategy for a capacity problem that does not exist. The vent tool is the cheapest item in either document and sits at position five. Customer files live in /tmp, filed as an "ops decision."
The Executor — Monday morning
The plan is fine; the sequencing has a hole and a collision. (1) Kill the M1b blocker in an hour: persistent dir, move appPath, deploy script. (2) Vent tool first: a day of work, catches the silent weeks. (3) Script the logo-saga replay before M2 or nothing can be declared done. Merge the two roadmaps into one list. Cut PID balancing; static per-project affinity is twenty lines. R2 is hours of Caddy work, detached from the agent: parallel with M0. M4 is the only true multi-week stage; everything else is smaller than the doc makes it look.

Peer review highlights

What the reviewers converged on (5/5)
  • Strongest response: First Principles, unanimously: the only response that converts the shared diagnosis into a design rule (no new guard surfaces before observability) and a concrete resequencing.
  • Biggest blind spots: the Executor's "the plan is fine" (never tests the premise; treats /tmp as an hour of mkdir) and the Expansionist's revenue framing (builds sellable surfaces on a platform that cannot detect its own breakage; would market a reliability metric produced by instruments that already lied).
  • Council-wide misses: live-tenant migration safety (no rollback, shadow period, or abort criteria anywhere); the vent tool's ~1/6 catch rate vs the synthetic canary's ~6/6; the /tmp move itself being the data-loss risk; no staging tenant, replay corpus, size estimates, or kill criteria, making the plan unfalsifiable.
